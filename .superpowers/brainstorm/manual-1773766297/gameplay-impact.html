What This Means for Strategy

1

Corners are KING

6 lines vs 3 for everything else — exactly 2× more valuable. The opening battle for corners defines the game. In 3×3×3 there was a middle tier (face-centers at 4 lines); in 4×4×4 it's binary: corners (6) vs everything else (3).

2

No Space Diagonals = Simpler Threat Model

All 4 space diagonals are eliminated by the center block. Strategy becomes purely planar — axis-aligned rows and 2D face diagonals only. No "through the whole cube" threats to track. This actually makes the game more learnable despite the bigger board.

3

Scoring is Harder: Lines of 4 vs Lines of 3

Completing 4-in-a-row with an opponent blocking is significantly harder than 3-in-a-row. Expect lower scoring games with more defensive play. Each completed line feels like a real achievement. Score might be like 3-2 instead of 8-6.

4

Games Are 2× Longer but Feel Different

28 moves per player vs 13. But with 0.86 lines/cell (vs 1.38), many moves are "positioning" rather than direct threats. The game shifts from tactical (every move matters in 3×3×3) to positional (build advantages over many moves). More chess-like.

5

AI Needs a New Brain

56 playable cells makes minimax infeasible (even with alpha-beta). The Normal heuristic CPU could adapt (just update wins/board), but Super Hard needs a new approach: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or deep heuristic evaluation. Still beatable, but no longer "perfect."
